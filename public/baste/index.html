Research · 04

Counterculture levers.

What works

Cancellation jiu-jitsu — suppression is the single most reliable sales catalyst observed (CMT pull → 108K/day; FTA “Ain't No Rock and Roll” 1M+ X views). ✓

Fan-army mobilization — coordinated release-day buying (MacDonald model). Baste already ran it: the “Hunter Got High” push to #1.

Parallel-economy commerce — merch as belonging, not memorabilia (Daily Wire; 39% gross margins at scale).

Sponsor-integrated releases — conservative brands co-fund campaigns (Patriot Mobile × Rachel Holt).

Cautionary tales

Outrage decays in ~a week (Aldean) unless catalog depth converts curiosity into fandom (Anthony).

Artists resent being weaponized (Anthony) — story and craft lead; politics as context, not costume.

The real return

VIP buyers are 5–15% of capacity but ~15–30% of ticket revenue. And every activation funnels to owned audience — QR on merch tables, free-download cards, VIP registration, photo-by-email. The show pays once; the list pays forever.

Research · 06

Superfan & D2C economics.

~20% of US listeners are “superfans,” spending ~80% more per month (Luminate). ○

One superfan spending $50–200/yr outweighs 10,000 casual streamers. ○

200–500 superfans = $3–15K/mo on Patreon-style tiers. ○

Physical = 25.9% of indie revenue (vs 16.4% global) — superfans buy vinyl/collectibles (ORCA). ○

Streaming pays ~$0.003–0.005/stream → 1M streams ≈ $3–5K before splits. That's why D2C. ○

Indie economics: ~$236K invested/artist; $1 in → $1.77 revenue; artist Spotify followers +44% (2023→25), per ORCA. ○
